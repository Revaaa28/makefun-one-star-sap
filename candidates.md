# Candidates — "One Star, Written in Sap"

## Judge brief, in order

1. A one-line one-star review from a houseplant about its human owner, with the plant's grievance at the centre.
2. Funnier — a surprising turn / sharp grievance.
3. Sounds like a plant writing — water, the fern getting watered daily, the plant's name tag.
4. A genuine one-star review: clear complaint plus the reason for one star, under 280 chars.
5. Fresh, specific detail, WITHOUT stock phrases like "drama queen", dramatic wilting, or generic neglect.

## Anticipated strong rivals

- "Drama queen wilting" clichés — a plant theatrically wilting, fainting, or playing dead for attention; stock phrasing the brief explicitly steers away from.
- Plain "you never water me" — a direct neglect complaint with no turn, no comparison, and no specific detail; true, but generic and forgettable.

## Candidate A — CHOSEN

> One star. The human waters the fern daily and me when he remembers my name, which is never. I am a pothos. My name is on the tag.

Rationale: a specific plant grievance — watered only occasionally while the fern is watered daily, and only when the human remembers its name, which is never. The closing turn "My name is on the tag" is sharp and in plant voice: the information was never hidden, it was attached to the plant the whole time. No banned stock phrases.

## Candidate B

> One star. She moved me six inches left for 'better light' and now I face a wall. I photosynthesize out of spite.

Loses: "photosynthesize out of spite" is a good turn, but A's grievance is sharper and more specific — the daily-watered fern and the name on the tag give A a comparison and a sting B does not match.

## Candidate C

> One star. She bought me for the aesthetic, put me in a dark corner, and blamed my leaves. The leaves were fine. The corner was a closet.

Loses: a good closing sting, but "blamed my leaves" is less sharp than "My name is on the tag" — C's reveal is about the room, A's reveal is about the plant itself and lands harder in plant voice.
